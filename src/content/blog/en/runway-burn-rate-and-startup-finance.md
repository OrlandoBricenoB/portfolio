Startups rarely die from a bad idea. They die from **running out of cash**. These six concepts tell you how much time you have and whether your business model works.

## Gross margin

```
Gross margin = (Revenue − COGS) / Revenue
```

COGS are the direct costs of delivering the product: hosting, payment fees, support, third-party APIs (including AI model costs). SaaS typically sits at **70–85%**. If yours is far lower, every new customer makes growth more expensive than it looks.

## Burn rate

**Burn** is the cash the company loses each month.

```
Net burn = Total expenses − Revenue
```

Gross burn is total spending; net burn is what actually leaves your bank account. Track net burn.

## Runway

```
Runway (months) = Cash in bank ÷ Net burn
```

$120,000 in the bank and $10,000 net burn per month is **12 months of runway**. Rules of thumb: start fundraising with **9–12 months** left; under 6 months you negotiate from weakness. Remember the number moves: if you hire, runway shrinks.

## Default alive vs. default dead

Paul Graham's test: at your **current growth and spending**, do you reach profitability before the money ends?

- **Default alive:** yes. You control your future.
- **Default dead:** no. You must raise money or cut costs.

Run this once a quarter, with honest growth assumptions.

## Rule of 40

For maturing SaaS:

```
Annual growth rate (%) + EBITDA or free cash flow margin (%) ≥ 40%
```

Growing 60% while losing 15% scores 45: fine. Growing 10% and breaking even scores 10: you are neither efficient nor fast. It balances growth against profit.

## Ramen profitable

**Ramen profitable** means revenue covers the founders' basic living costs. It does not make the company rich; it removes the pressure. You can negotiate with investors, say no to bad deals, or never raise at all.

## A monthly finance routine

1. Update cash, burn and runway.
2. Check gross margin per customer segment.
3. Re-run the default alive test.
4. Decide: cut, hold or invest.
