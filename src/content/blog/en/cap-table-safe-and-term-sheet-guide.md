Before you take money or give equity, you need to read the documents. This is a plain-language glossary, not legal advice. Always have a startup lawyer review what you sign.

## Cap table and dilution

The **cap table (capitalization table)** lists who owns what: founders, investors, employees, share types, and how ownership changes after each round.

**Dilution** is the drop in your percentage when new shares are issued. Owning 100% of a company and selling 20% leaves you 80%. Dilution is not bad if the company grows more than the slice you gave away.

## Pre-money vs. post-money

- **Pre-money valuation:** value before the investment.
- **Post-money valuation:** pre-money + capital invested.

**Example:** pre-money $4M, invest $1M → post-money $5M. The investor owns 1 ÷ 5 = **20%**.

## SAFE and convertible notes

- **SAFE (Simple Agreement for Future Equity):** created by Y Combinator to invest quickly without setting a valuation. It converts into shares in a future round.
- **Convertible note:** short-term debt that converts to equity at the next round, usually with interest.

Both use two protections for early investors:

- **Valuation cap:** a ceiling on the valuation at which they convert.
- **Discount:** a percentage off the next round's price (e.g. 20%).

**Example:** $100k SAFE, $5M cap. If the next round values you at $10M, the SAFE converts as if the company were worth $5M, so the investor gets twice the shares.

## Term sheet

A **term sheet** is a non-binding summary of the main financial and control terms before the final contracts. Read it for more than price:

- Valuation and amount.
- Board seats and veto rights.
- Liquidation preference.
- Option pool size.

## Vesting and cliff

**Vesting** means you earn your shares over time. Standard: **4 years with a 1-year cliff**. Leave before month 12 and you get 0%; at month 12 you receive 25%; the rest accrues monthly. It protects the company, and you, from a co-founder who disappears.

## ESOP

The **ESOP (Employee Stock Option Pool)** is a percentage of shares reserved to attract and reward future key hires, typically 10–15%. Investors often require it to be created **before** their money comes in, so it dilutes founders, not them.

## Liquidation preference

The right of investors to get paid **first** in a sale or liquidation, usually **1x** their investment, before founders receive anything. Prefer **non-participating 1x**. Watch for multiples above 1x or "participating" clauses.

## Exit

An **exit** is the liquidity event where founders and investors sell: an **acquisition (M&A)** or an **IPO**. Most exits are acquisitions. Know your cap table well enough to calculate what you would take home at different sale prices.

## Before you sign

1. Model your dilution across the next two rounds.
2. Understand every cap and discount in your SAFEs.
3. Set vesting for all co-founders from day one.
4. Read the term sheet's liquidation clauses twice.
5. Hire a startup lawyer.
